# Mentat

**Play the 1992 adventure-strategy classic *Dune* (CD version) on today's
computers, phones and tablets, without an emulator.**

Mentat is a free, open-source, from-scratch engine for the original game.
Bring your own copy of the CD version: Mentat reads its `DUNE.DAT` and
`DNCDPRG.EXE` and plays the game exactly as the original does, with the same story, screens, music
and voices, scaled sharply to any screen and with gamepad, mouse, keyboard
and touch controls.

> [!NOTE]
> **Status: early work.** Mentat plays the whole opening as the original
> does, in time with its music as the AdLib Gold card played it, in stereo
> with the card's surround sound: the logos, Irulan's spoken
> introduction with subtitles, the title, the flights over the desert, the
> palace and the sietch with the characters speaking with moving lips, the
> Harkonnens and their attack, with the original's fades, dissolves and
> zoom. Like the original it starts over until you press a key; then come
> the credits and the pages that tell the story, read by the narrator,
> and the palace at dawn. The game itself has begun: you start in the
> throne room with Leto, with the control panel, the clock that runs day
> and night, the palace's music and the mouse pointer, you can walk
> through the palace with the arrows, and you can talk with the people you
> meet: their face over the room and their own recorded voice, saying
> what fits how far the story has come. People also speak up by
> themselves when you come in or head for a door, and may stop you
> going, and those who follow you walk with you from room to room. Where
> a Harkonnen captain is held, each of them can try once to overpower the
> prisoner. "See Dune map" opens the map of
> the planet around you with the places you know, and you can move it
> with the arrows; a click on a place shows its label, with what it is,
> its name and, for places you know, the battle there or its equipment.
> The small planet beside it opens the globe, which turns with your
> place marked, and a click on it takes you to the map of that area.
> "See results" on the globe shows the day, your charisma,
> the areas you hold, your spice and the men on each side, with the planet
> coloured by who holds it, and "Save game" and "Load game" keep your
> game, in the same files as the original. In Paul's room you can look in
> the mirror, where Paul's face shows how long the game has gone on, and
> start again, save, load or leave the game from there. In the palace's entrance you
> can take an ornithopter and fly to a place you know: it takes off, the
> desert goes by with a small map of your way in the corner, and you land
> at the place, or skip straight there. A click on the desert flies off
> that way, steered with the arrows. A companion on board points out a
> hidden sietch you fly past, and you are warned when you enter the
> Harkonnens' zone; fly on and they catch you, and the game ends with the
> original's flashes, video and words, where you can start again, load a
> game or stop. Later in the story you can also call a
> worm, out in the desert or at a place under attack: choose a place on
> the map over the view, the worm rises from the sand and you ride it
> there, unseen by the Harkonnens. You can also walk out of a place into the desert, step by
> step, and wait there for evening or morning; walk too far and the sun
> gets the better of you, and you wake up back where you came from. In a
> sietch you meet its Fremen, who can agree to work
> for you. Their chief then takes your orders on the map, answering in a
> box with his face and his own voice: he tells you about his troop, and you can have it
> specialize in spice, the army or ecology, send an army troop to spy out
> the nearest hidden Harkonnen fort and then attack it, have an ecology
> troop build a wind-trap, give it equipment the sietch keeps
> or take some back, send it to fetch the equipment it lacks from the
> nearest place that has it, or send it somewhere: point at a
> place on the small map of the regions, and if the chief agrees the troop
> sets off and walks there hour by hour, its mark crossing the map. The
> prospectors can be given up to three sietches to visit in turn. A
> troop that reaches a Harkonnen fort, or a place being fought over,
> where nobody else is takes it. As Jessica teaches Paul her powers he
> reaches his troops from further off: "CONTACT FREMEN TROOPS" on the map
> then gives orders to troops far away, and "NEXT TROOP" goes from one to
> the next. Taking a
> Harkonnen fort raises your charisma and your troops' spirits, and once
> every fort of a region is yours, the Harkonnens' land there becomes
> yours. Hour by
> hour your troops work: they mine spice into your stock (and a harvester
> may meet a worm), prospect places, train for the army, build wind-traps
> and make the desert green. Send them to attack a Harkonnen place and
> they fight it out hour by hour: they may win it, flee, or lose a place
> of yours. The Harkonnens grow stronger and attack your sietches, the
> green you planted fades, and each day the spice and land accounts are
> kept. When the Harkonnens attack the place you are in, you see the
> battle from there, and can launch a massive attack or fight on for a
> whole day. The map shows your troops' marks, by what they do. Once the
> story gets that far, "See spice density" on the map opens that small
> map of the regions you know, coloured by their spice or, with a click on
> its bar, by the work of your troops there; point at a place to see who
> is there, or click it to move the map to it. While you wait in a room,
> news from your troops reaches you: the room blurs and a chief's face
> appears in a shimmering vision, showing the place on a small map; when
> the one with news is in the room with you, they tell you themselves. As the
> story moves on, doors in the palace open, people move to other rooms,
> new places become known and news arrives. At moments of the story a
> scene plays: the family gathers in a room and each speaks in turn while
> you click "Continue...". The first Fremen chief to work for you shows you the
spice map and how it will be kept up to date. In the palace's message room you can view the
news sent to you: the sender's hologram rises over the device and speaks.
When the Emperor demands spice, Duncan shows you the stock and proposes
amounts to send: answer ACCEPT, REFUSE or ARGUE; take him to the message
room and the spice goes, carried off by a frigate on the room's device, and the Emperor's mood and his next demand follow
from how much he got. Stilgar offers you the water of life, which only a
leader with enough charisma survives, and, when the time comes, asks
whether to launch the last attack on the Harkonnens' palace.
In a village the smuggler offers equipment and names his price: accept it
and it goes on his bill, or argue and he may come down; Duncan later asks
whether to pay the bill from the spice stock.
In the palace, the middle of the arrows shows a plan of its rooms and who
is in them. The "Mixer Panel" sets the volume and balance of the voices and
the music (and of the music while someone speaks), tests a voice, switches
the language (American, English, French, German, Italian, Spanish or
Fremen) and the subtitles, turns the music off, or plays it as a CD would,
in order or shuffled. Mentat's own options (F2, or Start on a gamepad)
choose which of the CD's arrangements of the music plays: AdLib, AdLib
Gold (the default) or Roland MT-32. The MT-32 is emulated (with Munt) and
needs your own MT-32 ROM files (the control ROM and the PCM ROM); none come
with Mentat. Add them as described under [Add your game](#add-your-game). Reach the Harkonnens' throne room and the story ends as the
original's does: the last scene, "THE END" and the cast, after which the
game starts again from its opening.

Mentat is not affiliated with or endorsed by the makers or publishers of the
original game or the owners of the *Dune* name. It contains none of the
original game's files: you need your own copy.

## Download

Get the newest build from the
[releases page](https://github.com/NilioDev/Mentat/releases):

- **Linux:** the `.AppImage`. Make it executable (`chmod +x`, or
  right-click, Properties, Allow executing file as program) and
  double-click it.
- **Windows:** the `windows-x64.zip`. Unpack it and start `Mentat.exe`.
- **macOS:** the `macos` zip. Unpack it and open `Mentat.app` (the first
  time: right-click, Open, since it is not signed).

## Add your game

The easiest way: start Mentat and give it your CD. Its game files screen
shows what it needs to play and what it can use besides. Drop the CD image
(`.iso` or `.bin`), or the CD itself, on the window, or press "Add files..."
to pick it. Mentat copies the two files it needs, `DUNE.DAT` and
`DNCDPRG.EXE`, to its own folder; press "Play" to start. Next time it finds
them there and starts at once. You can also start it with the image:
`./Mentat path/to/game.iso`.

For the Roland MT-32 music, add your MT-32 ROM files the same way, together
with the CD or later: drop them on the window at any time, or open "Game
files..." in Mentat's options (F2, or Start on a gamepad). Mentat knows them
by their contents, so their names do not matter, and keeps them as
`MT32_CONTROL.ROM` and `MT32_PCM.ROM` (or the CM-32L's) beside the game
files.

Mentat keeps the copied files where your system keeps settings for programs:

- Windows: `%APPDATA%\NilioDev\Mentat\gamedata`
- macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/NilioDev/Mentat/gamedata`
- Linux: `~/.local/share/NilioDev/Mentat/gamedata`

Or copy the two files from the root of your CD yourself, into a folder named
`gamedata` next to the program (for the AppImage: next to the `.AppImage`
file), or start it with the folder: `./Mentat path/to/folder`.

## Saved games

Mentat keeps your games where your system keeps settings for programs:

- Windows: `%APPDATA%\NilioDev\Mentat\saves`
- macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/NilioDev/Mentat/saves`
- Linux: `~/.local/share/NilioDev/Mentat/saves`

They are the original's own files, `DUNE37S1.SAV` and `DUNE37S2.SAV`, so
you can copy games saved in the original into that folder and play them on,
and the original can load the games you save in Mentat.

## Controls

With a gamepad, the left stick moves the pointer, and the direction pad
jumps it to the next line of the list, arrow, button, person, place on
the map, the small maps or the globe, or knob of the mixer panel that way.
It works as soon as you plug it in: A and X click, B and Y are the right
mouse button, Back does nothing (so you never quit by accident), and any
other button acts as a key. If you want another layout, put a file
`gamepad.txt` in the folder that holds `saves` (above), with lines such as
`b = click` or `back = quit`; [docs/gamepad.md](docs/gamepad.md) lists
every button and action.
